# 3A. Audited Functional Scope and Cross-Module Contracts

The implementation shall treat the following as the ten mandatory core modules: OPD, IPD, Laboratory Information System (LIS), Radiology Information System (RIS), Emergency, OT Management, ICU, Pharmacy Management, EMR and Insurance. Shared platform capabilities are implemented once and consumed by these modules.

### Functional scope requirements

- **OPD** shall register a patient to a department/service (for example Medicine, Surgery, ENT, Dental), manage consultation and orders, and support admission from OPD into IPD.
- **IPD** shall manage department-wise bed pools, ward/nursing-station workflows, nursing notes, vitals, doctor's notes, discharge summary, detailed MAR, medication orders, laboratory/imaging orders, transfers and ICU-transfer initiation.
- **LIS** shall provide complete order-to-result lifecycle support including accessioning, barcode/sample management, collection/rejection, processing, analyzer interfaces, QC, validation, reference ranges, critical values, amendment and report release.
- **RIS** shall provide complete imaging order/scheduling/worklist/reporting functionality with DICOM/PACS integration, critical findings, verification and result availability in EMR.
- **Emergency** shall provide triage, treatment, diagnostics, resuscitation, observation, admission, discharge, transfer/referral and configured MLC workflows.
- **OT** shall accept cases from configured procedural departments including Surgery, ENT, Dental and others; an authorized IPD user shall be able to schedule OT from the IPD patient context.
- **ICU** shall support full critical-care workflow including transfer from IPD/Emergency, ICU bed management, observations, flowsheets, infusions, device/ventilator documentation, critical events, step-down and discharge.
- **Pharmacy** shall receive OPD prescriptions and IPD/ICU/Emergency medication orders automatically into dispensing work queues without manual re-entry.
- **EMR** shall present a longitudinal, provenance-aware record containing data generated by OPD, IPD, Emergency, ICU, Pharmacy, LIS, RIS, OT and other configured clinical sources.
- **Insurance** shall support government/state/central schemes and private payers with eligibility, pre-authorisation, packages, claims, queries, rejections, resubmission, settlement and reconciliation.

### Canonical identity requirement

There is exactly one canonical patient master per tenant under the configured identity policy. A department change, encounter creation, admission, transfer, investigation, pharmacy transaction or insurance transaction shall not create a second patient master.

### Department-aware registration

`registration.department_id` and `registration.service_id` are mandatory for department-based OPD encounters unless the workflow is explicitly marked as emergency/unspecified by configuration. The patient identity is separate from the encounter/service context.

### Cross-module order contract

Every order shall contain at least:

- tenant_id
- facility_id
- patient_id
- encounter_id
- source_module
- source_department_id
- ordering_practitioner_id
- order_type
- priority
- status
- ordered_at
- authorization context

Downstream fulfilment records must reference the source order id.

### Cross-module linkage examples

```text
OPD consultation
  ├── prescription ──────────> Pharmacy
  ├── lab order ─────────────> LIS
  ├── imaging order ─────────> RIS
  └── admission request ─────> IPD

IPD stay
  ├── medication order ───────> Pharmacy ─────> MAR
  ├── lab order ──────────────> LIS
  ├── imaging order ──────────> RIS
  ├── ICU transfer request ──> ICU
  └── OT request ─────────────> OT

Emergency
  ├── medication/order ───────> Pharmacy/LIS/RIS
  └── admission/transfer ─────> IPD/ICU
```

No downstream module may require routine manual re-entry of source clinical data that already exists in the authoritative transaction.

# 4. Architectural Principles

## 4.1 Recommended baseline

The implementation should use:

- Type-safe backend services.
- API-first design.
- PostgreSQL or equivalent ACID relational database for transactional domains.
- Object storage for documents and large files.
- Redis or equivalent for caching and short-lived state.
- Search engine for patient/global search if scale requires it.
- Message broker/queue for asynchronous events.
- Central identity provider or equivalent identity service.
- Observability stack with logs, metrics and traces.

Technology choices are locked where explicitly stated in this SRS (including self-hosted Supabase/PostgreSQL, NestJS, Next.js/shadcn/ui and React Native/Expo). Other infrastructure implementations may vary only when they preserve the defined interfaces, security controls and operational requirements.

## 4.2 Service decomposition

The system should begin as a modular monolith or well-bounded service architecture rather than immediately fragmenting every domain into microservices.

Recommended domains:

- Identity & Tenancy.
- Organization.
- Patient/MPI.
- Appointments.
- Clinical/EMR.
- Orders/Results.
- Pharmacy.
- Inventory/Procurement.
- IPD/Bed.
- Nursing.
- Emergency.
- OT.
- Billing/RCM.
- Quality.
- Communication.
- Integration.
- Analytics.
- AI.
- Audit.

A later move to independently deployable services should occur only where scale, team ownership or isolation justifies it.

# 6. Multi-Tenancy Requirements

## 6.1 Tenant topology

The canonical shared-SaaS model shall be:

`Tenant → Facility → Department → Location → Resource`

A patient belongs to exactly one tenant and may have encounters at multiple facilities within that tenant. A patient must never belong to more than one tenant.

### 6.2 Scope inheritance

Access checks shall resolve:

`user → tenant memberships → facility memberships → department/resource scope → action permission`

A user with tenant membership but no facility or department scope shall not automatically gain access to all clinical data.

### 6.3 Database enforcement

Every tenant-owned table shall contain `tenant_id` unless a formally documented shared/global reference table is approved. RLS policies shall enforce tenant boundaries and, where applicable, facility/department boundaries. High-volume tables may be partitioned after measured scale thresholds are reached, but partitioning must not weaken RLS or auditability.

# 10. Database Requirements

## 10.1 General

- Primary keys shall be opaque globally unique identifiers such as UUIDs/ULIDs where practical.
- Human-readable numbers such as UHID, invoice number and accession number shall be separate business identifiers.
- Foreign keys shall be enforced for transactional integrity.
- Soft deletion shall be used only where legally/operationally appropriate; clinical and financial records generally require immutable history rather than deletion.
- Created/updated timestamps shall be stored in UTC.
- User-visible timestamps shall be converted to the facility timezone.
- Record versions or optimistic-lock fields shall exist on mutable high-value entities.

## 10.2 Clinical immutability

Signed/finalized clinical records shall not be destructively overwritten.

Corrections must create amendment/version events with:

- Original value.
- New value.
- Reason.
- User.
- Timestamp.
- Approval if required.

# 10A. Self-Hosted Supabase Requirements

The production baseline shall use self-hosted Supabase as a data platform, not as the sole business-logic layer.

### Required Supabase components

- PostgreSQL for transactional data.
- Supabase Auth for authentication/JWT issuance where selected.
- PostgREST only for approved simple data-access use cases; the NestJS application API remains canonical.
- Realtime for suitable non-authoritative UI updates.
- Storage for documents/media where appropriate.

### Business backend boundary

NestJS shall own business transactions, orchestration, domain validation, authorization decisions, clinical-safety checks and cross-module workflows.

The normal authenticated request path shall not expose a Supabase service-role key to clients. Supabase's service-role/secret credentials bypass RLS and must remain server-side. Normal business access should use restricted database credentials and transaction-scoped tenant/user context or an equivalent RLS-safe pattern. Supabase documentation recommends RLS for exposed tables and explicitly states that service-role keys bypass RLS and must be kept server-side. (https://supabase.com/docs/guides/database/postgres/row-level-security)

### RLS requirements

Every tenant-owned exposed table shall have RLS enabled. Policies must validate tenant and, where applicable, facility/department/resource scope. `TO authenticated` is not sufficient by itself; authorization policies must bind rows to the user's permitted scope.

For NestJS direct PostgreSQL access, tenant context must be established **inside each transaction** and cleared/replaced before reuse of a pooled connection. Connection-pool leakage of tenant context is a release-blocking security defect.

### Self-hosted operational responsibility

The deployment must separately implement:

- backup and restore
- off-host backup copies
- restore testing
- monitoring/alerting
- OS/container patching
- HA/scaling strategy
- TLS/ingress
- secret management
- log retention
- DR runbooks

Supabase's self-hosting documentation explicitly places these responsibilities on the operator and notes that several managed-platform capabilities are unavailable when self-hosting. (https://supabase.com/docs/guides/self-hosting)

# 14. Orders and Results Requirements

Orders must have clear lifecycle states.

Recommended order state:

`DRAFT → ORDERED → ACCEPTED → IN_PROCESS → COMPLETED → CANCELLED`

Result state:

`DRAFT → ENTERED → VALIDATED → RELEASED → AMENDED`

Critical-result state, where applicable:

`IDENTIFIED → NOTIFIED → ACKNOWLEDGED → CLOSED`

The system shall record user, time and communication method for critical-result handling.

### ABDM

The ABDM adapter shall follow the current ABDM production/sandbox API version, milestones and India-specific implementation profiles in force at onboarding. External data interchange shall use the currently required ABDM/NRCeS FHIR profiles rather than assuming that the newest generic HL7 FHIR release is automatically the ABDM contract.

### NHCX

Insurance integration shall maintain a dedicated NHCX adapter boundary. NHCX should be treated as a claims-exchange rail, not as a replacement for the internal payer/claim domain. The application shall preserve the internal claim version and map it to current NHCX specifications.

### DPDP

Privacy controls shall be designed to support the applicable DPDP Act/Rules obligations, including notice/consent or other permitted processing bases, security safeguards, breach handling, data-principal rights workflows, retention and processor governance as applicable. Legal interpretation remains deployment-specific and shall be reviewed with qualified counsel.

### Clinical terminology

The terminology layer shall support versioned code systems and mappings rather than storing free-text only. The implementation should support configurable use of ICD, SNOMED CT, LOINC and other required terminology/coding systems as appropriate to the use case and Indian implementation profile.

- Critical security vulnerability.
- Broken tenant isolation.
- Missing migration rollback/forward strategy.
- Data-loss risk.
- Unrecoverable failed transaction.
- Broken audit for regulated/high-risk workflow.
- Failed disaster-recovery test for release-impacting change.

# 100. Final Engineering Principle

The HIMS shall be built so that modules are replaceable but the patient, organization, identity, workflow, audit and interoperability foundations remain stable.

The product must therefore avoid the failure mode of traditional HIMS implementations: a collection of tightly coupled screens that happen to share a database.

The target architecture is a **workflow-driven, API-first, auditable hospital operating platform**.
